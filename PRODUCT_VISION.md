# Work Ledger Product Vision

**Status:** Product direction  
**Last substantive review:** 16 August 2026

## The future we want

AI makes it possible to change not only how quickly work is performed, but what work exists, who can do it, what becomes reusable, and which processes no longer need to survive in their current form.

Most organizations cannot see that transformation clearly. They can see model spend, seat counts, isolated prompts, shipped artifacts, or anecdotal success. Those are fragments. They do not show **how the work actually happened**, how practice is changing, or which parts of one person's learning deserve to become shared capability.

Work Ledger exists to make that visible.

> **Work Ledger turns evidence about how work actually happens into better ways of working.**

## The user promise

> **Understand how your work is changing, what appears to work better, and what deserves to be reused, improved, automated, shared, or eliminated.**

The immediate value begins with one person's own corpus. The broader value emerges as the circle of observation expands.

## The expanding circles

### 1. This work

Understand a single session or unit of work:

- what happened;
- what it cost;
- which tools and approaches were used;
- what was built;
- where effort repeated or failed;
- what evidence exists about the outcome.

### 2. My recurring work

Understand related work across sessions:

- what keeps recurring;
- where context is rebuilt unnecessarily;
- which artifacts are reused;
- which activities consume disproportionate effort;
- where candidates for skills, tools, automation, or deterministic substitution emerge.

### 3. My practice over time

Understand the trajectory of how a person works:

- what they do differently now;
- which practices appear more efficient;
- which appear more effective;
- which create greater downstream impact;
- what has become reusable capability;
- what they appear to have learned.

A defining experience is:

> **Show me how my work has changed.**

This is both useful in its own right and a validation surface. The person should be able to inspect the evidence, recognize themselves in the analysis, and correct what Work Ledger has misunderstood.

### 4. How we work differently

With explicit consent and appropriate governance, compare patterns across people—not to rank them, but to understand variation and comparative advantage.

The system should help reveal questions such as:

- Where is one person unusually effective or efficient?
- What behaviors appear to contribute?
- Where do several people independently converge on similar practice?
- Which successful practices are role-specific or expertise-dependent?
- Which approaches appear complementary rather than substitutable?

The output should be a **map of work styles, behaviors, efficiency, effectiveness, and impact**, not a leaderboard.

### 5. What we can learn from one another

Use overlapping evidence to identify candidate shared capability:

- reusable artifacts;
- prompts or methods worth preserving;
- skills;
- deterministic tools;
- workflows;
- review practices;
- conventions;
- process changes.

The objective is not to impose one person's dogma on a team. It is to understand what is idiosyncratic, what is genuinely transportable, and what multiple people's experience suggests could become better shared practice.

### 6. How the work itself should change

The final circle is process redesign.

Once Work Ledger understands enough about the work, it should help ask:

- What should be automated?
- What should become deterministic?
- What should become shared infrastructure?
- What should be simplified?
- What should be recomposed around new capability?
- What should stop being done entirely?

The destination is not cheaper AI usage. It is better work and greater leverage from what people and machines learn together.

## Product definition

> **Work Ledger is an evidence-backed work intelligence system that reconstructs how AI-assisted work happens, learns from patterns across work and people, and identifies what should be reused, improved, automated, standardized, redesigned, or eliminated.**

Today the product begins with local Claude Code transcripts because they provide unusually rich evidence of real work without requiring a new telemetry stack. That is the current substrate and adoption surface, not the ultimate conceptual boundary of the product.

## Why a person should care

The first reason to care is not organizational governance. It is recognition and learning:

> **This understands how I work. It can show me how I have changed and where I am getting better.**

That trust creates permission for the next circle:

> **If it can understand my work credibly, perhaps it can help us understand how we work differently and what we can learn from one another.**

The desired progression is:

> **understand my work → understand how my work is changing → understand how others work → understand what we can learn from one another → improve how the team works**

## Relationship to Wingman and Tricorder

The three products have distinct centers of gravity:

- **Wingman is person-centric:** who the person is, what they value and demonstrate, and how they can exercise better professional judgment.
- **Work Ledger is work-centric:** how work actually happens, how practice changes, and what should improve or become reusable.
- **Tricorder is team-practice-centric:** what teams build and how review, correction, and collaboration help them get to good.

They may eventually exchange evidence or capabilities. They should not blur their organizing questions.

## What success looks like

Work Ledger succeeds when people and teams can see their work with enough fidelity to change it deliberately.

A successful user should be able to say:

- I understand what my AI-assisted work actually consumes and produces.
- I can see how my practice has changed rather than relying on memory or anecdote.
- I can distinguish lower cost from greater effectiveness and impact.
- I can identify recurring work that should become reusable capability.
- I can see what other people do differently without reducing them to a score.
- We can learn from multiple people's practice rather than standardizing around whoever speaks loudest or arrived first.
- We can make evidence-backed decisions about what to automate, tool, standardize, redesign, or stop doing.

That is the future Work Ledger is trying to create.
